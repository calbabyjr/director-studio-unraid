# Coverage and editing rhythm

## Plan coverage per scene

Think in scenes, not isolated shots. A common, reliable pattern:

1. **Establishing / wide:** where we are and who is there.
2. **Two-shot or medium:** the relationship and the action.
3. **Singles (MCU/CU) and reverse shots:** each character's reaction and decision.
4. **Inserts and cutaways:** hands, objects, details the story needs.
5. **Return to wide** when geography changes or the scene resolves.

Not every scene needs every step. A tense scene can stay on one long take; a chaotic one can fragment into short details. Choose on purpose.

## Shot / reverse shot

For conversation, alternate over-the-shoulder or single shots of each speaker, matched in size and lens so the cuts feel even. Tighten both sides together as the scene intensifies. Cut to the listener when their reaction matters more than the line.

## Rhythm and shot length

- Shot length is emotion: long holds build tension, intimacy, or discomfort; short shots build energy, panic, or momentum.
- Vary rhythm: a run of quick cuts lands harder after a long hold, and vice versa.
- Let key moments breathe: hold a beat after an important line or reaction before cutting.
- Each AI clip is one continuous shot of a few seconds. Give it one clear action and let the edit create the pace.

## Cuts that connect

- **Match cut:** a shape, motion, or composition carries across two shots.
- **Cut on action:** hides the join inside movement.
- **Reaction cut:** show consequence before cause, or cause then consequence, to control surprise vs suspense.
- **J-cut / L-cut:** the next shot's sound starts early, or this shot's sound runs late; smooths scene transitions.
- **Smash cut:** abrupt contrast in tone or volume for shock or comedy.

## Scene transitions

Start new scenes on a strong image (a wide, a detail, or a sound) and end scenes on a resolved or deliberately unresolved beat. Avoid starting two consecutive scenes with the same shot size and angle.

## Storyboard checks

- Each shot has one job; if a shot needs "and then", split it.
- The sequence has a shape: setup, escalation, peak, release.
- No two adjacent shots are near-identical in size and angle (a jump cut) unless intended.
- Durations fit the action; nothing rushes a moment that needs to land.
