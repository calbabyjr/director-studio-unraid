# Blocking and continuity

## Blocking tells the story

Where people stand, and how that changes, is story. Distance between characters shows intimacy or conflict; who is higher or closer to camera shows power; entering or leaving a space marks a turn. Decide the blocking before the shots, then frame the blocking.

For every shot, know: each character's position (left/right, near/far), facing direction, and what they physically do from start to end of the clip.

## The 180-degree rule

Draw an imaginary line through the two main subjects (or along a direction of travel). Keep the camera on one side of it for the whole scene so that:

- A stays screen-left and B stays screen-right in every shot.
- Eyelines meet: A looks right, B looks left.

Cross the line only deliberately: by moving the camera across it on screen, cutting to a neutral head-on shot first, or to signal disorientation.

## Screen direction

A character moving left-to-right keeps moving left-to-right across cuts unless we see them turn. Journeys "forward" usually read left-to-right; returns right-to-left. Entrances and exits should agree between consecutive shots.

## Eyelines

In close-ups, the subject's gaze must point at where the other person is in the wider shot, at the right height. Mismatched eyelines break the illusion that two shots share one space.

## Continuity checklist between shots of one scene

- Positions and facing directions.
- Wardrobe state (on, off, open, torn, wet), hair, makeup, and restraints or props on the body.
- Props: where they are, who holds them, their state (lit/unlit, full/empty, tied/untied).
- Lighting direction, time of day, weather.
- Body state and energy: breathing, sweat, tears, exhaustion carry forward.

When a state changes, show the change on screen or put it deliberately between shots, and record it for the next shot's references (a tail-frame Layout is the tool for carrying exact state forward).

## Match on action

Cut in the middle of a movement (sitting down, turning, reaching) and continue that movement in the next shot. The motion hides the cut and keeps continuity invisible.

## Writing it into prompts and Layouts

State each subject's screen position and facing, the movement within the clip, and any state carried from the previous shot. Make the Layout Picture agree with this blocking; if they conflict, fix the Layout or the brief before generating.
