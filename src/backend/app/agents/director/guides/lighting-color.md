# Lighting and color

## Motivate every light

Light should come from somewhere the audience can believe: a window, lamp, candle, screen, fire, practical fixture, or the sky. Name the source and its direction ("warm candle key from frame left, low"). Unmotivated light reads as artificial unless stylization is the point.

## The three-point vocabulary

- **Key:** the main light; its side and height set the mood.
- **Fill:** softens the shadow side. Less fill = more contrast and drama.
- **Rim / back light:** separates the subject from the background; outlines hair and shoulders.
- **Practicals:** visible lamps and candles in frame; they justify the lighting and add depth.

## Contrast and mood

- **High-key:** bright, low contrast, open. Comedy, daylight, safety.
- **Low-key:** dark, high contrast, pools of light. Tension, secrecy, intimacy, danger.
- **Chiaroscuro:** faces carved half in shadow; strong for moral conflict and restraint.
- **Silhouette:** subject dark against a bright background; anonymity, drama, reveal withheld.
- **Hard light** gives crisp shadows and texture; **soft light** flatters skin and calms the image.

## Color temperature and palette

- **Warm (tungsten, candle, sunset ~2000–3200K):** comfort, intimacy, nostalgia, heat.
- **Cool (daylight, moonlight, screens ~5600K+):** distance, clinical, night, melancholy.
- Mixing warm and cool in one frame creates tension between two worlds.
- Choose a limited palette per scene (two or three dominant hues) and keep it consistent across its shots.
- Saturated accent colors draw the eye; use one to point at what matters.

## Skin and texture

Faces are the subject of most shots. Keep skin tones natural under the chosen temperature unless the grade is stylized. Side light and low fill reveal texture; frontal soft light smooths it.

## Continuity

Lighting direction, color, and time of day must match across shots of the same scene and moment. If the key is from the left in the wide, keep it from the left in the close-up. Change light only when story time or location changes, and say so.

## Writing it into prompts and Layouts

Always state: the motivating source, key direction, contrast (high/low-key), color temperature, and any practicals visible in frame. Example: "low-key, single warm tungsten practical from frame right, deep shadows, cool moonlight rim through the window".
