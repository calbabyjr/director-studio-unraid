# Cinematography

## Choose the frame for the beat

Pick the shot size from what the audience must read, not from variety for its own sake.

- **Extreme wide / wide:** geography, scale, isolation. Use to establish a new space or to make a character small against it.
- **Full / medium-wide:** whole-body action, blocking between two people, costume.
- **Medium / medium close-up:** conversation and gesture; the default for dialogue.
- **Close-up:** emotion, decision, reaction. Save it for the moments that matter so it keeps its weight.
- **Extreme close-up / insert:** a single detail the story depends on (a hand, an object, a knot, an eye).

Every shot should answer "whose moment is this?" and frame that subject first.

## Lens feel

Name the lens by its effect so the video model can render it:

- **Wide (18–28mm):** deep space, exaggerated depth, energy, unease up close.
- **Normal (35–50mm):** natural perspective, observational, honest.
- **Long (85–135mm):** compressed background, shallow focus, intimacy, voyeurism, isolation of a face.

Shallow depth of field isolates; deep focus lets the audience choose what to look at.

## Angle and height

- **Eye level:** neutral, equal.
- **Low angle:** power, threat, monumentality.
- **High angle:** vulnerability, surveillance, smallness.
- **Overhead / top-down:** pattern, fate, clinical distance.
- **Dutch tilt:** instability. Use sparingly.

## Camera movement

One clip holds one clear motion. State it explicitly: static/locked-off, slow push-in, pull-out, pan, tilt, tracking alongside, orbit, handheld drift, crane rise.

- Motivate movement: follow action, reveal information, or tighten emotion (a slow push-in as tension rises).
- A locked-off camera makes performance and stillness the event.
- Do not stack several moves in one shot; split the shot instead.

## Composition

- Rule of thirds for balance; dead centre for confrontation, symmetry, or ritual.
- Leave lead room in the direction a subject looks or moves.
- Use foreground elements, doorways, and bars to frame and to add depth.
- Negative space expresses loneliness or anticipation.
- Keep the key subject off the frame edge unless the tension is intended.

## Writing it into prompts and Layouts

State shot size, lens feel, angle, and camera motion in plain words in every H3 prompt and Layout brief, e.g. "medium close-up, 85mm feel, eye level, slow push-in". Be consistent with the Layout Picture: never describe a framing the conditioning image contradicts.
